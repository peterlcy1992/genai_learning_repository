# Stage 5 — Alignment and post-training

**Goal:** understand how a raw, pretrained next-token predictor becomes the
helpful, harmless, instruction-following **assistant** you actually interact
with. This is **post-training**, and it's arguably where most of the
*perceived* progress of the ChatGPT era came from.

---

## The problem

A base LLM (Stage 2–4) is trained to *continue text* from the internet. Ask it a
question and it might continue with more questions, because that's a plausible
web continuation. It is not trying to be helpful, truthful, or safe — those were
never in the objective. Post-training installs those behaviors.

Post-training has two classic stages, plus a newer simplification.

## 1. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) / instruction tuning

Collect high-quality **(instruction, ideal response)** pairs written by humans
and fine-tune the base model on them. The model learns the *format and habit* of
being a helpful assistant. **FLAN** (Wei et al., 2021) and **T0** showed that
instruction-tuning on many tasks generalizes to unseen instructions. SFT alone
gets you a decent assistant — but it can only imitate demonstrations, and
writing demonstrations for everything is expensive.

## 2. RLHF — Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback

The landmark is **InstructGPT** (Ouyang et al., 2022, *Training language models
to follow instructions with human feedback*, arXiv:2203.02155) — the direct
ancestor of ChatGPT. The pipeline:

1. **SFT** — as above, a supervised starting point.
2. **Reward model (RM)** — show humans several model responses to the same
   prompt and have them **rank** which is better. Train a separate model to
   predict these human preferences (a scalar "reward").
3. **RL optimization** — use reinforcement learning (**PPO**) to update the LLM
   to produce responses the reward model scores highly, with a **KL penalty**
   keeping it from drifting too far from the SFT model (so it doesn't
   "reward-hack" into gibberish).

The striking InstructGPT result: a **1.3B** RLHF model was preferred by humans
over the **175B** base GPT-3. *Alignment, not size,* made it useful. This is the
core lesson of the stage.

## 3. DPO and the "RL-free" simplification

RLHF with PPO is finicky — you juggle a reward model, a policy, a reference
model, and unstable RL. **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** (Rafailov et
al., 2023, arXiv:2305.18290) showed you can optimize the *same* preference
objective with a **simple classification-style loss directly on the preference
pairs** — no separate reward model, no RL loop. Much simpler and more stable, so
it (and relatives like **IPO**, **KTO**, **ORPO**) became the default for a lot
of open-model post-training. Frontier labs still use full RL pipelines (and
**online** methods) because they can squeeze out more, but DPO democratized
alignment.

## 4. Constitutional AI / RLAIF — scaling the feedback

Human labeling is a bottleneck. **Constitutional AI** (Bai et al., 2022,
Anthropic, arXiv:2212.08073) replaces much of the *human* feedback with **AI
feedback** guided by a written set of principles (a "constitution"): the model
critiques and revises its own outputs against the principles, and preferences
are generated by an AI judge (**RLAIF**). This scales oversight and gives
explicit, auditable control over behavior.

## Concepts you'll keep meeting
- **Reward hacking / specification gaming** — the model satisfies the letter of
  the reward while missing its intent (e.g. verbose, sycophantic answers that
  score well). A central alignment failure mode.
- **Sycophancy** — telling the user what they want to hear; a known RLHF
  side-effect.
- **Helpful/Harmless/Honest (HHH)** — the shorthand goal triad.
- **RLVR (RL from Verifiable Rewards)** — the bridge to Stage 7: for domains
  with checkable answers (math, code), you can skip the human/reward-model
  preference and reward *correctness* directly. This is the engine behind
  today's reasoning models.
