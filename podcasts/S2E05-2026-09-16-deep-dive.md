# Deep Dive: The Week AI Safety "Pacing" Became a Real Commitment

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's essay "We Must Pace the Frontier" argued that
the industry should deliberately slow capability gains so safety work can
keep pace — citing recursive self-improvement (models now helping build the
next generation) and the contested OpenAI agent-swarm incident as triggers,
and committing Anthropic to one unilateral step: permanent, employee-level
access for third-party evaluators.

Within a day, the AI Evaluator Forum published AEF-1, "Minimum Operating
Conditions for Independent Third-Party AI Evaluations" — a voluntary
cross-lab baseline for evaluator access, independence, and transparency —
cosigned by xAI, OpenAI, and Anthropic. And the following day, OpenAI's
global policy chief Chris Lehane confirmed publicly, for the first time,
that OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind have been coordinating on AI
safety since July, including discussions of third-party model evaluation and
a possible FINRA-style industry standards body.

This episode traces how a single lab CEO's proposal turned into a concrete,
ongoing, three-lab commitment in under a week — what "pacing the frontier"
actually means in practice, why evaluator access is the specific lever these
labs chose, and what's still unproven about whether voluntary coordination
between competitors can hold.
