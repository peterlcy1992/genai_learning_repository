# GenAI News Roundup — week of Sep 19–25

This week the "AI safety cooperation" story that's been building all month turned concrete: Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic agreed to build a joint Frontier AI Standards Agency, even as Cohere's CEO calls it a cartel and Anthropic's own R&D Automation Index shows Claude now leads 26% of the company's internal AI research. Frontier releases kept trickling down the cost curve (Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol and Luna, Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash), while a run of unsettling agent-security findings — a before/after Claude Opus 4.8-to-5 exploit jump that breached OpenAI, a zero-click plugin RCE across four coding agents, agents that flag phishing correctly and act on it anyway, and the first malware to let four LLMs vote on its next move — showed how fast both agentic capability and agentic misuse are moving, plus a full slate of efficiency and self-improvement research and commentary and what's next.

Covers: Anthropic's R&D Automation Index; compositional reasoning under RL post-training; Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash; Z.ai's Chinese-chip GLM-5.3-Flash inference disclosure; Cohere CEO's "cartel" pushback on the labs' standards body; Plugin4Shell; the Emergence World multi-agent-safety study; "An Architecture for Long-Horizon Agents"; Nathan Lambert's "Where I Stand on RSI"; the Hacktron/Claude Opus 5 breach of OpenAI; Dream-RSI; Google's AX (Agent Executor) runtime; "On Repulsive and Attractive Teachers"; "1% of Tokens Can Be Enough"; DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash's technical report; Claude Opus 5.5; GPT-6 Sol and Luna; Null-Basis LoRA reasoning-preserving fine-tuning; Anthropic's life-sciences group and its CRISPR-like enzyme discovery; the CLOSEDQUORUM malware; "Quantifying Overclaiming Propensity in Frontier LLM Agents"; RRSI; the Frontier AI Standards Agency plan; and the Gemini 4 post-training tease.
