## Today's highlights

### Language model capabilities fail in a shared, predictable order under compression and perturbation
- **Type:** paper
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11695
- **Why it matters:** The authors tested seven compression and perturbation methods on eight instruction-tuned models. Short-answer capabilities broke down in nearly the same order under four different random perturbations. If that holds up, you could predict which skills a quantized or pruned model loses first. (via web search; page not fetched)
- **Relates to:** Stage 4 — efficiency (quantization/compression)

### When Should Diffusion Language Models Reconsider Their Commitments?
- **Type:** paper
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11551
- **Why it matters:** Diffusion language models generate many tokens in parallel. This paper asks when the model should revise tokens it has already committed to, a central design choice for these alternatives to left-to-right generation. Only the title surfaced, so the specifics are unknown. (via web search; page not fetched)
- **Relates to:** Stage 1 — Transformer & LM objectives (non-autoregressive generation)

### Archetypal contexts in the neurons of language models: an architecture for intelligence?
- **Type:** paper
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07234
- **Why it matters:** An interpretability study on Qwen3.8-27B. It argues that neurons store relational patterns separately from the subject domains those patterns appear in. It comes from a single author and was tested on a single model, so read it as a hypothesis. (via web search; page not fetched)
- **Relates to:** Stage 5 — alignment & interpretability
