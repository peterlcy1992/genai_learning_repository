## Today's highlights

A lighter day. Today's top items are a cluster of early-November papers on making reasoning models cheaper. They come from the first days of the month but hadn't been logged until now.

### Can open-weight models shorten their reasoning by compacting their own earlier thoughts?
- **Type:** paper
- **Link:** https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.03852
- **Why it matters:** This paper tests whether open-weight reasoning models can shorten their traces by rewriting ("compacting") their own earlier thoughts, without any retraining. Long traces are what make reasoning gains expensive. The exact title is unverified because only an aggregator snippet surfaced. (via web search; page not fetched)
- **Relates to:** Stage 6 — reasoning models (test-time compute cost)

### LoCo: local preference learning for reasoning compression
- **Type:** paper
- **Link:** https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.04814
- **Why it matters:** LoCo uses preference learning at each step to decide whether more reasoning is needed, which aims to curb "overthinking." Code and models are promised. (via web search; page not fetched)
- **Relates to:** Stage 6 — reasoning models; Stage 3 — preference optimization

### LLM routing when strong reasoning models join the pool
- **Type:** paper
- **Link:** https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.04098
- **Why it matters:** This paper studies how routers that pick between cheap and expensive models behave once reasoning models are added, including the cost of runaway generations. It is a practical concern for multi-model systems. (via web search; page not fetched)
- **Relates to:** Stage 4 — efficiency; Stage 7 — applications
