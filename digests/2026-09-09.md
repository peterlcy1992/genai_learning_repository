### Fractal basins trap latent reasoning
- **Type:** paper
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04963
- **Why it matters:** Gives a mechanistic, physics-flavored explanation for why reasoning models spend longer on harder problems: they behave as dynamical systems with transient chaos, and the fractality of their decision basins grows with task difficulty across Sudoku, mazes, visual puzzles, and logic tasks.
- **Relates to:** Stage 7 — Frontier systems (reasoning models, test-time compute).

### Evaluation of Contextual Understanding in Large Language Models
- **Type:** paper
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09004
- **Why it matters:** Standard metrics like perplexity and BLEU can't distinguish genuine context integration from memorized pattern-matching. Proposes a knowledge-graph-based score (S3KG) and an error taxonomy for judging QA answers on whether the model actually used the context it was given.
- **Relates to:** Stage 3 — Scale and emergence (in-context learning, evaluation beyond benchmarks).

### Training-Free Halving of Activated Experts in Fine-Grained Mixture-of-Experts Models
- **Type:** paper
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04575
- **Why it matters:** Fine-grained MoE routers implicitly calibrate expert output strength to the training-time top-k, which is why cutting active experts at inference normally hurts quality. This paper's training-free fix lets you roughly halve activated experts — cheaper serving — without a retraining pass.
- **Relates to:** Stage 4 — Efficiency & building blocks (MoE).
